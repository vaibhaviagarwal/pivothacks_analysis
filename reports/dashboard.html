A hackathon rebuilt from its leftovers

Twelve hours in Waterloo left behind a registration export, a sheet of problem assignments and four Instagram posts. Nobody scanned a QR code, nobody tagged a link, and the platform's team fields were never filled in. This is what can still be recovered.

Applications arrived at about four a day until a post went out. The reel on 08.27 reached more people than anything else published, and landed one day before the window closed.

25Applications in two days, against 8.4 expected

Timing beat reach by a factor of three

The applications open post drew 25 applications. The reel nine days later reached 700 more people, collected 76 shares against 55, and produced +0.6 above baseline. The content worked. The date did not.

1.00/0.44Precision and recall on teammate pairs

Teams came out of a text box, at a price

With the platform's team fields empty, the only record of who worked with whom was free text. Matching those typed names against the roster never produced a false pair and found fewer than half the real ones, because the matcher refuses any name that could belong to more than one person.

5Competitors missing from the registration export

The denominator is wrong

5 of the 35 people who competed appear on the assignment sheet and nowhere in the export. The real pool was larger than 107, which makes the 74% acceptance rate a ceiling rather than a measurement.

130/88Median words written, accepted against denied

One field on the form told reviewers anything

Reviewers rated experience first, then project depth. But portfolios, resumes and LinkedIn profiles came from almost everyone who applied, so none of them separated anybody. The adaptability answer is the only field where accepted and denied applicants differ.

Never written down

Not gaps in the analysis. Measurements the event could have taken for free and did not, each one closing off a question permanently.

  • checked_in_atEmpty for all 107 rows. QR codes were generated and never scanned, so attendance is unknowable.
  • utm_sourcePresent on 10 of 107. Instagram likely drove around half the pool; the tracking accounts for six of them.
  • link_clicksNever collected. Shares measure amplification, not intent, so no post converts to an application on record.
  • review_scoreNot stored. Only the accept or deny survives, so the stated criteria cannot be checked against the ratings.
